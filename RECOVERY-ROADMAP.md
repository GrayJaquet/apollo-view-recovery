# Recovery roadmap

## Materials to recover

- Original VIEW FORTRAN source decks, listings, subroutine inventories, and revision histories.
- UNIVAC 1108 / FORTRAN V build and operating instructions, library dependencies, and graphics interfaces.
- Input card formats, trajectory and ephemeris conventions, coordinate frames, spacecraft-window geometry, and lunar terrain data.
- Original star catalogues and their identifiers, epochs, proper-motion conventions, magnitudes, and formatting.
- Reference inputs and outputs that could support historical validation.

The [1972 NASA technical report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19720017950/downloads/19720017950.pdf) is the principal verified description of VIEW. Its appendix describes catalogues of 391 and 1,078 stars. The five-page star table in [69-FM-197 Revision 1](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/19740073250.pdf), printed pages 291–295, contains 148 entries for Besselian 1970, including the first 37 navigation stars. That table is useful recovered data, but does not supply the complete catalogues described in 1972. No machine-readable historical VIEW star catalogue is included in this release.

## Next indexes and finding aids

These are proposed research tasks, not completed scrapes or confirmed holdings of VIEW source.

1. Parse the complete [JSC Document Index](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19900007273), correlating report numbers, authors, offices, and related titles.
2. Cross-reference the [Apollo Engineering and Technology Index supplement](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/NARASWoverflow/CorporateIndexSupplement.pdf) for contractor report numbers and MSC accessions.
3. Investigate the [UHCL JSC History Collection access points](https://uhcl.libguides.com/JSCHistoryCollection/AccessPoints) and [series descriptions](https://uhcl.libguides.com/JSCHistoryCollection/SeriesDesc), including availability of Apollo inventories and legacy index exports. An online finding aid is not proof that a report has public full text.
4. Trace [NARA Record Group 255](https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/255.html) series and accession descriptions. Reconcile historical website box labels with current containers before ordering reproductions.
5. Search NASA COSMIC software catalogues, [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/), [NTIS NTRL](https://ntrl.ntis.gov/), Virtual AGC, Internet Archive, and university finding aids for exact identifiers, authors, and computing terminology. VIEW's distribution through COSMIC remains unproven.

Use the [prioritized requests](potential_archival_requests.md) for specific document leads. Priorities reflect research value, not proven absence from the Internet or confirmed possession by an archive.

## Recording evidence

Keep original wording and source URLs alongside corrections. Record retrieval dates, report numbers, revision dates, page numbers, and hashes for recovered files. Distinguish direct mentions of VIEW, strong indirect connections, and speculation. Record unsuccessful searches without treating them as proof of nonexistence. Preserve authorship, task-monitor roles, and receipt dates separately.

Future source recovery should retain provenance and original bytes. Any inferred data, translated code, or recreated implementation should state its derivation and validation limits.
