# Apollo VIEW research findings

Research checked 17 October 2026 UTC. This catalog covers the accessible title-page archive, not all physical Apollo holdings. The original VIEW FORTRAN source has **not been located** in this work. No catalog title-page record has a confirmed explicit naming of VIEW as the program; 11 provisional works have strong indirect evidence and 73 have speculative relevance. The full ranked list contains 50 distinct provisional work identities, with the reason for each score and its image link.

## Verified technical anchor

[NASA TN D-6853 / MSC-S-323, Hyle and Lunde, June 1972](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19720017950/downloads/19720017950.pdf) is a verified public 19-page report. It identifies VIEW's UNIVAC 1108 / FORTRAN V implementation, Encke or Cowell trajectory integration, CRT and microfilm graphics, and Gemini rendezvous/docking ancestry. It discusses coordinate transformations, ephemerides and lunar perspectives. This is an external context report, not a fabricated match to a title-page image in this archive. It is not the source listing.

That report supplies a useful retrieval vocabulary: integrator and graphics portions, nonpowered trajectory state vectors, coordinate transformations, lunar/solar ephemerides, star catalogs, spacecraft windows, and lunar crater perspectives. Ask for program descriptions, deck inventories, routine names, input definitions and revision correspondence using those terms. Do not assume every UNIVAC, navigation or simulation report is part of VIEW.

## Strongest catalog connection

[69-FM-197, Revision 1, July 3 1969](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/19740073250.pdf) matches [the E157G-38 title scan](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/NARASWoverflow/TitlePageScans/E157G-38/E157G-38.tif.png). Its author leaf names Alfred N. Lunde. Its introduction credits G. B. Roush of the Computation and Analysis Division for the major analytical tool. The report contains crew viewing products and a star identification catalogue. This is strong indirect evidence linking the research people and products; the title scan itself does not name VIEW as a program.

The public copy has 321 PDF pages. A references divider is followed by blank pages near the end, so exact page-for-page archival completeness is not certified. Use the existing public report first; investigate any specific missing leaf separately. Its introduction says it supersedes 69-FM-168. The base and revision are therefore separate historical works even though their titles are similar.

[69-FM-107, April 22 1969, Apollo 10 May 18 launch](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap10fj/pdf/a10-views-from-sc-1969-05-18-launch-19690422.pdf) is also publicly available: 145 PDF pages, matching cover and author leaf. The legacy NASA path returned 404 in direct retrieval, but the Apollo Flight Journal mirror succeeded. The earlier 69-FM-74 for another launch date remains a separate availability question.

## Priorities before requesting reproductions

The [prioritized potential requests](potential_archival_requests.md) exclude all verified public copies across consolidated member records. Highest priorities are the Apollo 12–14 viewing reports and descent-visibility reports: 69-FM-264, 69-FM-297, 70-FM-4, 70-FM-38, 70-FM-188 and 70-FM-212. They may preserve evolving output conventions, input geometry, authorship and references. These are document-recovery priorities, not assertions that their full text is unavailable. The 69-FM-108 crater-visibility nomograph and the 69-FM-168 base viewing report are useful related targets. Search public repositories again using exact revision and launch date before commissioning scans.

For software ancestry, the E209H-113 memos describe TRW lunar-site perspective plotting: 68-7236.10-004 (March 6 1968), 68-7236.10-5 (March 25), and 68-7236.10-8 (May 10). The March material describes development/completion of a perspective-plotting capability, including Calcomp output. Their relationship to VIEW is **unproven**; a different contractor tool could be involved. Request supporting program documentation and task correspondence only after identity and availability checks.

The ORACLE-MAFIA sensitivity memo, 68.4711.4-43, establishes an adjacent UNIVAC 1108 software lead and cites a user manual. It does not establish visualization ancestry. OBSIM/NAVSIM/AGSIM manuals, the GLSAEG ephemeris generator description, and programming manuals are separately listed as software-documentation leads. Their presence is not evidence that original VIEW source survives among them. A. N. Lunde appears as a task monitor on the Apollo 6 abort plan; that role has not been mislabeled as authorship in the reviewed fields.

## Public copies and search coverage

[Verified public full text](verified_public_full_text.md) contains eight matched catalog works plus the external 1972 technical anchor. The matched works include 69-FM-107, 69-FM-197, MSC-EG-69-2, and the base 69-FM-326 star-horizon processor report with four separate changes. Change memos preserve their own identities/dates; a base-report citation is not a change's publication date. These reports are potential geometry/input or software context, not recovered VIEW source code.

Availability Checks records the exact search source, query, date, result type, URL, method and notes. Saved JSON responses and PDF hashes are under `catalog/research/`. Scope includes the complete saved Virtual AGC links index, NTRS identifier/title searches for 65 high-scoring image-derived records, Internet Archive metadata searches for 16 high-scoring records, Purdue archival catalog entries, Google Books bibliographic metadata, and NARA-focused public web searches. Machine links are only candidates until matched to an actual retrieved PDF. Similar Apollo mission titles generated false matches; those were rejected rather than counted as full text.

Purdue's entries for [69-FM-74](https://archives.lib.purdue.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/38476) and [69-FM-107](https://archives.lib.purdue.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/38313) are physical-holding descriptions, not report PDFs. [The Google Books entry for the Apollo 12 visibility report](https://books.google.com/books/about/Out_the_window_Visibility_During_Lunar_D.html?id=ms7_zgEACAAJ) is bibliographic. A JSC document index confirms 70-FM-188, but an index entry is not that report's full text. NARA checks used its publicly indexed web catalog; they were not an exhaustive search of unindexed finding aids or physical records. No-result and access-error records prove neither unavailability nor uniqueness.

## NARA Fort Worth retrieval information

Supply the archival entry/collection, historical box, exact filename/image URL, title, report numbers, date, revision and contributor roles from the catalog. Ask archivists to reconcile those with current containers. [The archive creator warns about subsequent reboxing](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/QuestForInfo.html); the online directory names are historical labels, not verified modern shelf locations. The two user-supplied finding-aid PDFs remain unchanged and have not been used to assert an unverified current box mapping.

## Quality limits

All 3,014 source images have inventory, preserved bytes, checksums, decoding, OCR evidence and structured records. Two recovered faint/handwritten scans still lack reliable complete transcription. Selected fields on 56 source records were reviewed; all records retain review flags for remaining uncertainty. The 46 boundary flags were visually inspected and showed single pages, including covers, tables, distribution leaves, changes and correspondence. Detection recall on unflagged pages is not certified. Provisional deduplication groups 87 additional references into 2,927 work records; every one of the 3,014 image-derived records remains in Page Records. This is complete processing coverage, not a claim that every title, author, identifier or historical-document identity is correct.
