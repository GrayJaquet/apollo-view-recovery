SUMMARY

This report presents the results to date on the selection and identi-
fication of landmarks for Apollo site LI-P-8. The techniques used in the
analysis and the goals of this task are described also. The selection of

landmarks is based on the following:

o Appearance and patterns of surface features as shown in vertical

and oblique Orbiter photography.

o Feature patterns as shown in out-of-the window views (perspective

plots).
o Visibility considerations,

o Availability of landmarks during descent and for different landing

points.

Results for TI-P-8 indicate that anticipated approach azimuths do not

strongly affect choice of landmarks, either for their effect on patterning

or on visibility of features. Sun elevation angie is a significant factor;

the maximum elevation angle for II-P-8 is about 11° resulting in several

otherwise useful features falling in the washout zone. A preliminary set

of landmark features and patterns has been identified for this site. These

patterns are useful for general orientation in the landing area and for

locating the actual landing site with respect to the nominal.

Due to the unexpected large number of features that had to be examined,

it was found necessary to develop a computer program to produce the per-

spective plots. This has delayed a complete analysis of LI-P-8; however,

This report presents the results to date on selection of landmarks for
Site TI-P-2. Since the last report (on Site LI-P-8) was issued, a Calcomp
program to produce perspective plots has been completed and was used for the
present study. The analysis for Site II-P-2 was performed for two launch
dates, chosen to represent extremes of sun elevation angles and as large a
range of approach azimuths as possible within the sun angle range constraint.
The two conditions were: sun angle 13°, approach azimuth 86° and sun ansle

6°, approach azimuth 102°. The results indicate that:

(1) For the 6° sun angle case, a set of about eight primary landmar

suffices to locate the landing point anywhere within the 99% ellipse.

(2) Many of the primary landmarks may not be visible for the 13° sun

angle case because they fall in the washout area.

(3) Almost all of the important landmarks fall outside the 99% ellipse,

consequently

o little information is available to indicate a touchdown within the
50% ellipse

o models for landmark recognition, if they are to be useful, must

Preliminary Report on Site II-P-13 BLDG MAIL STA. ExT.
R3 2004 66775
SUMMARY

This report presents preliminary results on visibility of landmarks
for Site II-P-13. Sun elevation angles of 6° and 11.5° and a single
approach azimuth of 88° were assumed. This site possesses a greater
number and more distinctive landmarks than the two sites previously
examined, II-P-8 and II-P-2, and the maximum sun angle has less
affect on landmark visibility than for the other sites. In addition,
a large crater, Maestlin G, is visibile through the right LM window
which can provide general orientation information at the maximum

sun elevation for a landing anywhere in the 99% ellipse. As for
previous sites, however, the primary landmarks fall outside the

99% ellipse area and many downrange landmarks fall in the washout

region for the high sun angle condition.
